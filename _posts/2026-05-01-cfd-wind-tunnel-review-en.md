Our review paper **["Complementary use of CFD simulations and wind tunnel tests in wind engineering: A practical classification and examples of their integration"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jweia.2026.106499)** has been published in *Journal of Wind Engineering and Industrial Aerodynamics*.

**Yoshihide Tominaga, Zitao Jiang**  
*Journal of Wind Engineering and Industrial Aerodynamics*, Vol. 276, 106499, 2026

[**Read the paper →**](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jweia.2026.106499)

Understanding airflow is fundamental to many problems in the built environment, including **wind around buildings, pedestrian wind environments, natural ventilation, and pollutant dispersion**. Two major approaches are commonly used to investigate these phenomena: **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)** and **wind tunnel testing**.

Rather than considering CFD and wind tunnel testing as competing approaches, this review discusses how their complementary use can improve the **accuracy, efficiency, and reliability** of wind engineering research.

The integration of CFD and wind tunnel testing is classified into four forms:

1. **Validation** — using wind tunnel measurements to validate CFD simulations
2. **Model improvement** — improving numerical models based on experimental evidence
3. **Multi-scale integration** — combining the two approaches across different spatial and temporal scales
4. **Wind tunnel test enhancement** — using CFD to support and improve the design and interpretation of wind tunnel experiments

This work was conducted in collaboration with **Prof. Yoshihide Tominaga of Niigata Institute of Technology**, the first author of the paper.

Our research group combines **CFD, wind tunnel experiments, and field measurements** to investigate airflow and environmental problems in buildings and cities.
