### 2026.08 | Publication
#### POD-Based Reduced-Order Modeling of Indoor Temperature Fields
Our paper **["POD-Based Reduced-Order Modeling of Indoor Temperature Fields in Impinging Jet Ventilation: Parameter-Based Prediction and Sparse Sensor Reconstruction"](https://doi.org/10.1002/2475-8876.70109)** has been published in *Japan Architectural Review*.
**Zitao Jiang, Tomohiro Kobayashi**
*Japan Architectural Review*, Vol. 9, Issue 1, 2026
[**Read the paper →**](https://doi.org/10.1002/2475-8876.70109)
This study applies **Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD)** to develop reduced-order models for rapid prediction and reconstruction of indoor temperature fields under impinging jet ventilation.
Two complementary approaches were investigated:
1. **Parameter-Based Prediction (POD-RBF)** — predicting the complete indoor temperature field from operating conditions such as inlet velocity and internal heat load.
2. **Sparse Sensor Reconstruction (POD-LSE)** — reconstructing the complete temperature field from measurements obtained by a limited number of temperature sensors.
The results demonstrate the potential of POD-based reduced-order modeling for efficient indoor environmental prediction without repeatedly performing computationally expensive CFD simulations. The sparse-sensor approach is particularly promising for future applications in **real-time environmental monitoring, HVAC control, and building digital twins**.

### 2026.05 | Publication
#### Complementary Use of CFD Simulations and Wind Tunnel Tests in Wind Engineering
Our review paper **["Complementary use of CFD simulations and wind tunnel tests in wind engineering: A practical classification and examples of their integration"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jweia.2026.106499)** has been published in *Journal of Wind Engineering and Industrial Aerodynamics*.
**Yoshihide Tominaga, Zitao Jiang**
*Journal of Wind Engineering and Industrial Aerodynamics*, Vol. 276, 106499, 2026
[**Read the paper →**](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jweia.2026.106499)
Understanding airflow is fundamental to many problems in the built environment, including **wind around buildings, pedestrian wind environments, natural ventilation, and pollutant dispersion**. Two major approaches are commonly used to investigate these phenomena: **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)** and **wind tunnel testing**.
Rather than considering CFD and wind tunnel testing as competing approaches, this review discusses how their complementary use can improve the **accuracy, efficiency, and robustness** of wind engineering research.
We propose a practical framework that classifies their integration into four forms:
1. **Validation** — using wind tunnel measurements to validate CFD simulations
2. **Model improvement** — improving numerical models based on experimental evidence
3. **Multi-scale integration** — combining the two approaches across different spatial and temporal scales
4. **Wind tunnel test enhancement** — using CFD to support and improve the design of wind tunnel experiments
This work was conducted in collaboration with **Prof. Yoshihide Tominaga of Niigata Institute of Technology**, who is the first author of the paper.
Our research group continues to combine **CFD, wind tunnel experiments, and field measurements** to investigate airflow and environmental problems in buildings and cities.
